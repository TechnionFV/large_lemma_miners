### Module Behavior and Property Meaning

The `BidirectionalCounter` is a parameterized module that implements a counter with both up and down counting capabilities. `upDown` determines the counting direction. When `upDown` is high, the counter increments, and when low, it decrements.
It is parameterized by:
- Width (default: 8 bits)
- MaxValue (default: 255)

On reset (rst), the counter is set to 0.
- If upDown == 1 and count < MaxValue, the counter increments.
- If upDown == 0 and count > 0, the counter decrements.
The counter saturates at the boundaries (0 and MaxValue) — it does not overflow or underflow.


The property being verified states that in the absence of resets, one of two things must happen:
- The `upDown` signal keeps toggling infinitely often, or
- Eventually, the `count` value stops changing permanently.
Put simply, if upDown ever stops toggling, then the counter must eventually stabilize.



### Proposed Lemmas:
We observe the following:
- The counter cannot exceed `MaxValue`. If `upDown` remains high from some point onward, the counter will eventually saturate at `MaxValue`.
- Likewise, the counter is bounded below by zero. If `upDown` remains low from some point onward, the counter will eventually stabilize at `0`.

Building on these invariants, we propose a lemma which strengthens the original property by explicitly characterizing the only two stable states the counter can reach when resets are absent and `upDown` stops toggling. It partitions the system's long-term behavior into three mutually exclusive outcomes:  
(1) `upDown` continues toggling forever, or
(2) the counter eventually reaches `MaxValue` and stays there, or  
(3) the counter eventually reaches `0` and stays there.

The lemma replaces the abstract ($past(count) == count) "stuck" condition with more specific terminal states (`count == 0` or `count == MaxValue`). These help reduce the reachable state space and are easier for model checkers to reason about.
The lemma also implies the original property. That property accepts two behaviors, in the absence of resets:
1. `upDown` keeps changing (toggling forever).
2 `upDown` stops changing from some point onward -> eventually `count` stops changing.
The lemma refines (2) by saying: if `upDown` stops toggling, then `count` eventually reaches one of two possible boundaries and remains there.
